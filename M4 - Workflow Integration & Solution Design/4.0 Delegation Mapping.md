### 1. Summary

The [Delegation Mapping: Redesigning Workflows with Claude Inside](https://anthropic-partners.skilljar.com/path/claude-certified-associate-foundations/workflow-integration-solution-design/486638/scorm/3d1npd3vxzw4l) module addresses a core architectural challenge: enterprise teams either over-delegate tasks to AI (accumulating unmonitored legal or financial risk) or under-utilize model capabilities by failing to structure repeatable delegation. Systems must decompose business processes step-by-step and assign explicit task ownership before embedding AI features.

**Core Concepts & CCDV-F Technical Takeaways**

* **The Three Mapping Criteria**: Every workflow step must be independently evaluated against three criteria:
    * **Reversibility**: The operational ease of undoing a step if executed incorrectly. High reversibility allows AI delegation; low reversibility mandates human ownership.
    * **Stakes**: The potential financial, legal, or operational cost of an error. High-stakes steps require human-retained gates or collaborative oversight.
    * **Accountability**: The non-delegable legal and professional responsibility for an outcome. Accountability never transfers to an AI model, regardless of drafting quality.


* **Three Operational Ownership Categories**:
    * **AI-Appropriate**: Low-stakes, highly reversible, mechanical tasks (e.g., clause extraction, playbook departure flagging) or numerical operations executed via code.
    * **Collaborative**: Iterative co-creation where the AI generates candidate drafts or redlines and human domain experts evaluate each edit.
    * **Human-Retained**: High-stakes, irreversible, or legally binding external actions (e.g., final contract sign-off, external email dispatch) requiring human authorization.


* **Feature-to-Step Alignment**:
    * **Skills**: Reusable configured skills should be attached to AI-appropriate steps to enforce standardized procedural rules.
    * **Code Execution**: Numerical and financial computations must be executed deterministically in code runtimes rather than estimated via natural language completions.
    * **Explicit Review Gates**: Human-retained steps must be architected as hard stop checkpoints in the pipeline.


* **Workflow Anti-Patterns**:
    * **Over-Delegation**: Assigning decision-making authority to AI based on high drafting quality rather than the underlying risk profile.
    * **Halo Delegation**: Delegating a downstream step to AI solely because a preceding step was executed successfully.
    * **Collapsing Collaborative into Automate**: Establishing a "human-in-the-loop" review gate on paper that is never actually staffed or enforced in production.
    * **Mapping the Tool vs. the Work**: Designing workflows around preferred software features instead of mapping actual underlying operational steps.

### 2. Clear & Simple Explanation

* **Map the Work, Not the Tool**: Before writing prompts or configuring APIs, list out every step of your business process and explicitly assign who owns what: the AI, a human, or both working together.
* **The 3 Evaluation Questions**:
    1. *Can we undo it easily?* (Reversibility)
    2. *How expensive is a mistake?* (Stakes)
    3. *Who gets blamed if it fails?* (Accountability)


* **Match Features to the Task**:
    * Use a **Skill** when Claude needs to follow a standard rulebook every time.
    * Use **Code Execution** when dealing with numbers, totals, or financial penalties (never rely on text generation for math).
    * Build **Review Gates** where humans must explicitly click "Approve" before an irreversible action happens.


* **Three Critical Mistakes to Avoid**:
    * *Drafting is not deciding:* Clean AI text does not mean the AI gets to make the final choice or sign the document.
    * *Don't assume success carries over:* Just because Claude extracted data perfectly in Step 1 does not mean it should make the final decision in Step 2 (*Halo Delegation*).
    * *Unstaffed gates are automated gates:* If you claim a human reviews the AI's work, but no human actually checks it, you have quietly built an unmonitored automated system.

### 3. Real-World Application

**Scenario: Automated Enterprise Procurement & Vendor Contract Onboarding**
A corporate legal and procurement department redesigns its vendor onboarding pipeline to process vendor Master Service Agreements (MSAs) and calculate financial liabilities.

```text
                        [ Inbound Vendor Contract / MSA ]
                                        │
                                        ▼
                   [ Step 1: Extract Clauses & Metadata ]
                   • Delegation: AI-Appropriate
                   • Feature: Configured Extraction Skill
                   • Rationale: Highly reversible, low stakes, mechanical
                                        │
                                        ▼
                 [ Step 2: Flag Playbook Departures ]
                 • Delegation: AI-Appropriate
                 • Feature: Contract Compliance Skill
                 • Rationale: Reversible; skill enforces strict playbook rules
                                        │
                                        ▼
             [ Step 3: Compute Financial Liability & Penalties ]
             • Delegation: AI-Appropriate (Code Execution)
             • Feature: Sandboxed Python Runtime
             • Rationale: Numerical math must be computed, not estimated
                                        │
                                        ▼
                   [ Step 4: Draft Redline & Rationale ]
                   • Delegation: Collaborative
                   • Feature: Multi-Draft Interactive Workspace
                   • Rationale: AI drafts revisions; human evaluates edits
                                        │
                                        ▼
             ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
             │                                                     │
  [ Step 5: Approve / Reject Redlines ]           [ Step 6: Sign & Dispatch Contract ]
  • Delegation: Human-Retained                      • Delegation: Human-Retained
  • Mechanism: Mandatory Review Gate                • Mechanism: External Execution Gate
  • Rationale: High stakes; accountability           • Rationale: Irreversible, legally binding
    does not delegate                                 external commitment

```

**Applied Architectural Principles:**

1. **Configured Skills & Code Execution**: Standardized rules are governed by a configured Skill in Steps 1 and 2, while financial penalty logic is isolated in a Code Execution runtime in Step 3.
2. **Collaborative Draft Gate**: Step 4 provides an interactive environment where human attorneys evaluate AI-suggested redlines rather than accepting them blindly.
3. **Un-Delegated Review Gates**: Steps 5 and 6 enforce strict human-retained checkpoints, ensuring no contract is approved or dispatched without legal sign-off.

### 4. Key Terms Note Section

* **Delegation Mapping**: The architectural practice of breaking a workflow into discrete steps and assigning ownership (AI-Appropriate, Collaborative, or Human-Retained) based on risk criteria.
* **Reversibility**: The operational ability to undo or recall an action if executed incorrectly.
* **Stakes**: The financial, legal, operational, or reputational cost incurred by an error at a specific workflow step.
* **Accountability**: The non-delegable legal and professional responsibility for an outcome.
* **AI-Appropriate Step**: Low-stakes, highly reversible, or mechanical sub-tasks assigned entirely to model execution.
* **Collaborative Step**: A human-in-the-loop workflow step where the AI generates candidate drafts and human experts evaluate and refine each edit.
* **Human-Retained Step**: High-stakes or irreversible decisions explicitly withheld from AI execution and reserved for human authorization.
* **Skill Integration**: Configuring reusable prompt boundaries and procedural rules to ensure consistent execution across repeated AI steps.
* **Code Execution Path**: Deterministic code execution in a runtime environment for processing quantitative or numerical steps.
* **Halo Delegation**: An anti-pattern where a workflow step is inappropriately delegated to AI simply because prior steps succeeded.
* **Collapsing Collaborative into Automate**: An anti-pattern where a designated human review gate is left unstaffed, converting a collaborative step into unmonitored automation.

### 5. Exam Practice

* **Question 1**
* An enterprise software team is designing an automated workflow for contract processing. The team plans to assign the step *"Compute financial exposure of a penalty clause"* to Claude. What is the recommended technical feature to pair with this step?
* A. A system prompt instruction requesting markdown table completions.
* B. A high-temperature API setting to generate multiple statistical estimates.
* C. A Code Execution environment to compute numbers deterministically.
* D. A collaborative human review gate with natural language explanation prompts.
---

* **Question 2**
* A lead developer notices that an automated vendor onboarding pipeline delegates the final contract signature to Claude because the model successfully extracted clauses, flagged playbook departures, and generated clean redline drafts. Which mapping error did the team commit?
* A. Over-delegation caused by Halo Delegation.
* B. Mapping the tool as opposed to the work.
* C. Collapsing collaborative steps into human-retained gates.
* D. Improper context window configuration.
---

* **Question 3**
* A workflow design document specifies that a legal redlining step is "Collaborative: AI drafts, human reviews each edit." However, in production, the engineering team routes the AI redline outputs directly to external vendors without requiring human review actions in the UI. How is this failure pattern classified?
* A. Feature mis-alignment.
* B. Deterministic drift.
* C. Prompt truncation error.
* D. Collapsing collaborative into automate.
---

* **Question 4**
* When evaluating whether a specific workflow step should be classified as **AI-Appropriate**, **Collaborative**, or **Human-Retained**, which three criteria must system architects use to judge the step?
* A. Token Cost, Model Latency, Context Length
* B. Reversibility, Stakes, Accountability
* C. Temperature, Top-P, System Prompt Length
* D. API Rate Limits, Data Size, Memory Overhead



---

**Answer Key & Explanations**

* **Question 1: Correct Answer – C**
* *Explanation:* Financial and numerical calculations must be computed deterministically using Code Execution rather than estimated via natural language completions.


* **Question 2: Correct Answer – A**
* *Explanation:* Passing an irreversible, high-accountability step (contract signing) to AI just because previous extraction and drafting steps went well is the classic definition of Over-Delegation driven by Halo Delegation.


* **Question 3: Correct Answer – D**
* *Explanation:* When a step is designated as collaborative ("AI drafts, human reviews") but the review gate is never enforced or staffed, the step quietly degrades into unmonitored automation.


* **Question 4: Correct Answer – B**
* *Explanation:* The Delegation Mapping framework evaluates every workflow step against three core criteria: **Reversibility** (can it be undone?), **Stakes** (what is the cost of error?), and **Accountability** (who is answerable for the outcome?).
